Urban tree inventories in Canadian municipalities

Alexander J.F. Martin, Tenley M. Conway, Lukas G. Olson & Emma S. Bacon

Journal of Forestry, advance online publication
DOI: 10.1007/s44392-026-00117-5

Abstract

Municipal tree inventories support urban forest planning and management, while also providing data for research on urban forest structure and equity. We surveyed Canadian urban municipalities to assess the state, development, and use of municipal tree inventories and their associations with municipal, environmental, and institutional characteristics. Among 135 responding municipalities, 77% reported an existing or in-progress tree inventory. Inventories were predominantly field based, digitized, and focused on street and park trees. Remote sensing, including for canopy cover maps, was used by 47.6% of municipalities with inventories, most of whom also had field-based inventories. Tree location, species identification, diameter at breast height, and tree condition were the most commonly recorded metrics, aligning with the predominant use of inventories for tree planting and maintenance activities. More than one-fifth of municipalities with inventories reported that their inventories were used for only a single need or purpose. The presence of a tree inventory was positively associated with municipal land area and a dedicated urban forestry budget, while more advanced inventory states and broader data collection and use were generally associated with greater urban forestry budgets and staffing levels. Municipal size was also associated with several inventory outcomes, whereas environmental characteristics were less consistently associated. Contemporary best practices should emphasize digital data management, stable spatial references such as geographic coordinates, using accepted taxonomic nomenclature, and preservation of past inventory data to support long-term monitoring and interoperability.

Volunteer engagement in Canadian urban forestry – A neoliberal fix or a complement to urban forestry staff?

Alexander J.F. Martin, Tenley M. Conway & Ashlynn Fleming

Urban Forestry & Urban Greening, 124, 129618
DOI: 10.1016/j.ufug.2026.129618

Abstract

Volunteers are a core component of municipal urban forestry. Most research in this area has focused on volunteer demographics, benefits, motivations, and barriers. Less is known about how municipalities themselves engage volunteers or whether volunteerism functions as a substitute for municipal capacity. In this Short Communication, we analyze volunteer-related questions from a national survey of Canadian municipal urban forestry programs to estimate the scale of volunteer participation and examine how population, climate, canopy cover, and urban forestry budget and staffing are associated with volunteer engagement and activities. Specifically, we examine whether volunteers are a substitute for adequate municipal staff and budgets or whether they primarily reflect community engagement efforts. We found that 52.6% of municipalities engaged volunteers in urban forestry activities. Volunteers participated either individually or through organizations, most commonly local environmental groups, school and youth groups, non-profit organizations, and private corporations. Volunteer efforts were concentrated in naturalization and park tree planting, aligning with the training municipalities most often provided. Volunteer engagement was more common in municipalities with larger populations, higher urban forestry staffing levels, and larger urban forestry budgets. Municipalities with more staff and greater financial resources also engaged more volunteers overall, supported a broader range of training opportunities, and partnered with a more diverse set of organizations, while urban forestry budget inadequacy had no significant effect. These findings suggest that volunteer engagement in Canadian urban forestry functions primarily as a complement to municipal capacity and as a means for community engagement, rather than as a neoliberal substitute for professional staff or adequate public funding.

Conducting a national urban forest management survey – Insights from the 2025 Canadian Urban Forest Census

Alexander J.F. Martin, Tenley M. Conway, Emma S. Bacon, Andrew Almas, Richard J. Hauer, Greg King, Andrew K. Koeser & Amory Ngan

Urban Forestry & Urban Greening, 121, 129459
DOI: 10.1016/j.ufug.2026.129459

Abstract

National surveys of municipal urban foresters provide an opportunity to summarize and benchmark urban forestry programs across municipalities. Such surveys – often termed “urban forest censuses” – have been conducted internationally, including in Canada, the United States, Ireland, New Zealand, Sweden, and the United Kingdom. To encourage the expansion of such surveys, this Short Communication details the methods used in the 2025 Canadian Urban Forest Census (French: Recensement des forêts urbaines canadiennes), which obtained a 40% response rate among all Canadian urban municipalities, cities, regions, and towns. Designed with the support of urban forestry researchers across Canada, our bilingual, online survey was disseminated using a mixed-mode approach that included promotion on a national urban forest electronic mailing list and LinkedIn; distribution of the online survey via a QR code on mailed postcards; and distribution and redistribution of the online survey link through email. We found that distribution and redistribution through email was the most successful approach, with few responses initiated through postal mail invitations. A higher response rate was achieved when we could obtain direct contact information for urban foresters or directors of parks, public works, planning, or related departments. Our results suggest that piloting surveys with municipal foresters and prioritizing email-based dissemination to identified contacts are key strategies for achieving high-quality, cost-effective response rates in urban forest management surveys.
