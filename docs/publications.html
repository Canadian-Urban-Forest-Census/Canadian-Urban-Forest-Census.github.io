Urban tree inventories in Canadian municipalities

Alexander J.F. Martin, Tenley M. Conway, Lukas G. Olson & Emma S. Bacon

Journal of Forestry, advance online publication

Seventy-seven per cent of responding municipalities hold an existing or in-progress tree inventory, nearly all of them digitized and focused on street and park trees. A budget line predicts whether an inventory exists; budget size and staffing predict whether it stays current and how many ways it gets used.

Volunteer engagement in Canadian urban forestry – A neoliberal fix or a complement to urban forestry staff?

Alexander J.F. Martin, Tenley M. Conway & Ashlynn Fleming

Urban Forestry & Urban Greening, 124, 129618

Just over half of responding municipalities engage volunteers in urban forestry. Volunteer programs turn out to be most active where municipal budgets and staffing are strongest, and perceived budget inadequacy predicts nothing — evidence that volunteering complements professional capacity rather than substituting for it.

Conducting a national urban forest management survey – Insights from the 2025 Canadian Urban Forest Census

Alexander J.F. Martin, Tenley M. Conway, Emma S. Bacon, Andrew Almas, Richard J. Hauer, Greg King, Andrew K. Koeser & Amory Ngan

Urban Forestry & Urban Greening, 121, 129459

How the census was designed, who was eligible, and how it reached a 40% response rate across 343 municipalities. Email outreach to named individuals and specific departments substantially outperformed general municipal inboxes and mailed postcards — a replicable framework for national surveys elsewhere.
