Across Canada, hundreds of municipalities are responsible for managing trees along streets, in parks, and in natural areas. Urban forestry programs vary considerably between these municipalities, from who is responsible for tree care to how programs are funded and what work is carried out. Until recently, there was little national information available to comapre these programs.

The Canadian Urban Forest Census (in French, Recensement des forêts urbaines canadiennes) is a bilingual survey of municipal urban forestry programs across the country. In 2025, we invited Canada's 343 urban municipalities to describe their tree care programs, including staffing, budgets, management practices, and commmunity involvement. A total of 137 municipalities responded, representing a response rate of 40%.

Why a census

National surveys allow municipalities to compare their urban forestry programs with those of similarly sized communities. They also help researchers and non-profit organizations identify gaps in knowledge and areas where further research or investment may be needed. Similar surveys have been conducted in the United States since the 1970s, and more recently in Ireland, New Zealand, Sweden, and the United Kingdom. Previous Canadian surveys were generally more limited in scope, often focusing on larger municipalities or lacking the statistical analyses needed to account for differences between communities.

The Canadian Urban Forest Census was open to municipalities meeting Statistics Canada's urban population criteria of at least 1,000 residents and a population density of at least 400 people per square kilometre. We applied these criteria to all 5,161 census subdivisions in Canada, excluding Indigenous communities with different governance structures and accounting for municipal amalgamations since the 2021 Census. This produced a list of 343 eligible municipalities across all ten provinces, ranging from four each in Prince Edward Island and Newfoundland and Labrador to 88 in Québec. Their populations ranged from a few thousand to several million.

What the survey asked

The Canadian Urban Forest Census was adapted from an American urban forestry survey first conducted in 2014. It covered eight areas of municipal urban forestry, including staffing, budgets, planning, tree management, inventories, and community involvement.

Municipal urban forestry managers in British Columbia, Ontario, and Québec reviewed the questionnaire. Their feedback led to several changes, including simpler budget questions and additional questions about street trees, which are often managed separately from trees in natural areas. The final survey contained 106 questions.

Further information about the questionnaire, eligibility criteria, and survey distribution is available on the Methods page.
