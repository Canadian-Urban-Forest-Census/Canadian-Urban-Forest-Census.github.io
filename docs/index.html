Across Canada, hundreds of municipalities manage trees along streets and in parks and natural areas. Urban forestry programs vary between these municipalities, including who is responsible, what resources are made available, and the manner in which the work is conducted. Until recently, Canada lacked a contemporary picture and benchmarks of these programs.

The Canadian Urban Forest Census (in French, Recensement des forêts urbaines canadiennes) is a bilingual survey of municipal urban forestry programs across the country. In 2025 we invited Canada's 343 urban municipalities to describe their tree care programs, ranging from community support to economics and staffing to arboricultural operations. One hundred and thirty-seven municipalities responded — a 40% response rate.

Why a census

National surveys provide municipalities an opportunity to compare their own program against those of a similar size. They also show researchers and non-profit organizations where there is need for further study and investment. Similar surveys have been run in the United States since the 1970s, and more recently in Ireland, New Zealand, Sweden and the United Kingdom. Canada’s previous efforts were more limited in scope, often restricted to larger municipalities, or did not conduct statistical modelling, limiting their efficacy for benchmarking.

The Canadian Urban Forest Census surveyed Canada's 343 urban municipalities, defined by Statistics Canada as having a population of at least 1,000 people with a density of at least 400 people per square kilometre. We applied this definition to all 5,161 census subdivisions in Canada, excluding Indigenous communities that operate under different governance structures and merging municipalities that have amalgameted since the 2021 Census of Population. This process produced 343 eligible municipalities in every province, from four each in Prince Edward Island and Newfoundland and Labrador to 88 in Québec, and ranging in population from a few thousand to several million.

What the survey asked

Developed based on a 2014 survey in the US, the Canadian Urban Forest Census included questions across eight sections, covering community support, budget, staffing, planning, management, tree inventories, and volunteers and partnerships. It was reviewed by municipal urban forestry managers in British Columbia, Ontario and Québec. Their suggestions included simplifying the budget questions and adding a set of questions specific to street trees, since responsibility for trees in natural areas often varies by municipality. Following this process, the survey included 106 questions.

Full detail on the survey instrument, eligibility, and how the survey was distributed is on the Methods page.
